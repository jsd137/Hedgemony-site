Hedgemony™ User Guide

Hedgemony runs a lawn care business from your pocket: your schedule, your clients, and what you're owed. Everything works with no signal, so a dead spot at the back of a neighborhood never stops you. This guide walks the app the way you'd walk a work day.

Your clients never see or install anything. Texts come from your own phone number, and invoices arrive as normal PDFs. To them, it's just you, but organized.

1. Get your clients in

Open the Clients tab and tap the green +. You can add one client by hand, with their address and schedule on a single screen, or import your whole list at once.

  • From Contacts: pick the people who are clients. Names, phones, and addresses come across.
  • From a spreadsheet: a CSV exported from Excel, Numbers, or Google Sheets. Only a name column is required, and column order doesn't matter. You see a preview of exactly what will be created, with any problems flagged, before anything is saved. There's a template in the import screen if you'd rather start from that.

Give a client a schedule (weekly, every 2 weeks, or monthly) and the next ten weeks fill in by themselves. Change the schedule later and the app shows you exactly which visits will move before you confirm.

2. Run your day

The Today tab is the screen the app opens to. The green header answers the only question that matters: how's the day going. Stops left, money earned, money still out there.

  • Go: tap the little green arrow next to any address to open Maps and drive there.
  • Done: swipe a card right to complete it at the plan price, one motion, no typing.
  • Skip or move: swipe left.
  • Reorder: press and hold a card, then drag it to where it belongs in your route. The order sticks.
  • Left over from a rained-out day? It shows up on top in orange with a Do it today button, so nothing silently falls through.

Gate codes and dog warnings sit right on the card, because that's when you need them.

3. Complete a job

Tap a card to open the job. Everything is prefilled from the client's plan, so the common case is two taps: open, then Done.

  • Upcharges: tap a chip (Weed eating, Bushes, Beds, Edging, Cleanup) to add a line, then type the price. + Add makes a custom line.
  • Timer: tap Start when you get out of the truck. It keeps counting with the phone in your pocket, and Stop then Resume keeps the total if you take a break mid-job. Or just type the minutes. Over time this builds your average per yard, which is how you'll know which clients are underpriced.
  • Notes: anything worth remembering ("gate was locked, skipped the back").

The big button at the bottom shows the total it's about to charge. That tap is what puts the money on the client's balance.

Made a mistake? Open the completed job and use Undo completion. The charge comes off the books and the stop goes back on today's list.

4. The week

The Schedule tab shows the week as day cards with each day's stop count and dollar total. Arrows move between weeks.

  • Move a stop: drag it to another day, or drop it onto another stop to land in that exact position. A green bar shows where it will land. Moving one visit never shifts the client's rotation, so a biweekly client stays biweekly.
  • Rained out: open the day and use Move all to push every remaining stop to another day at once.
  • One-off work: cleanups, mulch jobs, anything outside the regular rotation, from the menu in the corner.
  • Tap a stop in a day to open that client's profile.

5. Text your clients

The evening before a work day, open the Schedule menu and tap Text tomorrow's clients. Hedgemony queues tomorrow's stops with a message already written for each one, using your template. Tap Send on each and it opens in Messages addressed to that one client. You send it, from your number, so it reads like you and not a robot.

The wording lives in Settings → Message templates. Tokens like {{name}} and {{day}} fill in automatically per client. The same system writes balance reminder texts from the Money tab.

Nothing ever sends by itself. No timers, no automation, no surprise messages to your clients.

6. Get paid

The Money tab keeps the score: the total owed to you, split by how old it is. Current, 30, 60, 90 plus. Each client who owes shows how long they've owed and gives you three moves:

  • Text reminder: a friendly balance text, written for you, sent by you.
  • Invoice: pick which visits go on it, and the app makes a clean PDF with your logo. Send it by text, email, or AirDrop.
  • Record payment: cash, check, Venmo, Zelle, or card. The balance updates instantly.

Invoices are frozen once issued. If you fix a price afterwards, the client's balance updates but the document they received never changes out from under them.

What your client receives

Set your logo, Venmo line, and mailing address once in Settings → Business profile, and every invoice tells the client exactly how to pay you.

7. Know your clients

Tap any client to see the whole relationship on one screen: balance and status up top, Text and Call and Directions one tap away, their property with its schedule and gate code, recent money activity, and lifetime numbers at the bottom.

  • See all visits opens the full service history: every visit, what was done, what it cost, how long it took.
  • The ••• menu holds contact details, notes, birthdays, status changes (including marking a client lost, with the reason), and extra properties for clients with more than one yard.

8. Winter and spring

When the season ends, open the Schedule menu and choose Season pause. Pick the clients (everyone, by default) and their upcoming visits clear from the planner. History and balances stay put.

In spring, the same screen brings everyone back in one tap. The break isn't backfilled and every client keeps their place in the rotation, so a biweekly client returns on the same alternating weeks they left on.

9. Your business, your data

Settings → Business profile holds your name, phone, logo, sales tax rate if you charge it, and the how-to-pay details for invoices.

Your data lives on your phone and works fully offline. If you're signed into iCloud it backs up automatically to your private iCloud, and Settings says plainly whether backup is on. Prepare export gives you your clients, visits, and money as plain CSV files any spreadsheet can open, and the client file imports straight back into a fresh install. No accounts, no tracking, no servers.
